01
Phishing & Smishing

Fake Emails and Text Messages

What It Is

Phishing (via email) and smishing (via SMS) occur when scammers pretend to be a trusted organization, such as a bank, courier, school, or government agency, to trick you into revealing passwords, credit card numbers, or OTPs.

Warning Signs

  • Urgent threats ("Account locked in 24 hours")
  • Misspelled website links (e.g., gcash-verify-ph.com)
  • Generic greetings
  • Requests for sensitive personal info

How to Prevent It

  • Always inspect the sender's address and website URL carefully.
  • Bookmark official banking and school websites instead of searching for them or clicking message links.

If it already happened

  1. Disconnect from the internet immediately.
  2. Change your affected account password using a different clean device.
  3. Enable 2FA.
  4. Contact your bank or e-wallet provider to temporarily freeze your account.
02
Account Takeover

Credential Stuffing

What It Is

When a website suffers a data breach, hackers collect leaked email and password pairs and automatically test them on other popular platforms (social media, e-wallets, shopping apps). If you reuse passwords, one leak compromises all your accounts.

Warning Signs

  • Password reset emails you did not request
  • Login alerts from unfamiliar locations or devices
  • Friends telling you your account is sending spam

How to Prevent It

  • Create long, unique passwords (at least 12 characters mixing letters, numbers, and symbols) for every important account.
  • Turn on app-based Two-Factor Authentication (ex. Google Authenticator).

If it already happened

  1. Use the platform's official "Account Recovery" or "Hacked Account" page immediately.
  2. Revoke access to unknown third-party apps.
  3. Log out of all active sessions in your security settings.
  4. Warn your contacts not to send money to your compromised profile.
03
Malware & Malicious APK

Harmful Software

What It Is

Malware is harmful software designed to spy on your screen, steal saved passwords, or lock your files for ransom. On Android devices in the Philippines, malware frequently spreads through pirated apps or .apk installer files sent via messaging apps.

Warning Signs

  • Your phone battery drains unusually fast
  • Unknown apps appear on your screen
  • Constant pop-up ads appear outside your browser
  • Your device overheats while idle

How to Prevent It

  • Only install applications from official stores (Google Play Store or Apple App Store).
  • Disable "Install Unknown Apps" in your phone settings.
  • Avoid pirated software cracks.
  • Never download .apk files sent via chat.

If it already happened

  1. Turn on Airplane Mode to stop the malware from sending your data.
  2. Boot your device into Safe Mode.
  3. Uninstall suspicious or recently added apps.
  4. Run a scan using reputable security software.
  5. For severe infections, back up essential photos/documents and perform a factory reset.
04
Public Wi-Fi & USB

Unsecured Networks and Charging Risks

What It Is

Free public Wi-Fi hotspots in cafes, malls, and airports often lack encryption, allowing attackers on the same network to intercept unencrypted data. Additionally, tampered public USB charging ports can secretly transfer data or install malware onto connected phones (known as "juice jacking").

Warning Signs

  • Duplicate Wi-Fi network names (e.g., "Free_Mall_WiFi" alongside "Free_Mall_WiFi_5G")
  • Your phone prompting "Trust This Computer?" when plugged into a public charging station

How to Prevent It

  • Avoid accessing bank accounts, e-wallets, or sensitive portals while connected to public Wi-Fi.
  • Switch to mobile data for sensitive transactions.
  • Carry your own wall adapter or power bank instead of plugging directly into public USB ports.

If it already happened

  1. Disconnect from the suspicious network immediately.
  2. Select "Forget This Network" in your Wi-Fi settings.
  3. Change passwords for any accounts you logged into during that session.
