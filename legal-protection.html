Know Your Digital Rights

Understanding the Philippine laws that protect your personal data, penalize cybercriminals, and hold organizations accountable.

RA 10175
Cybercrime Prevention

Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012

RA 10175 is the primary law in the Philippines that defines and penalizes crimes committed through computer systems and the internet.

Key Offenses Covered

  • Illegal Access & Hacking: Entering a computer system, portal, or account without permission.
  • Data & System Interference: Intentionally damaging, deleting, altering, or disrupting computer data or networks.
  • Computer-Related Identity Theft & Fraud: Stealing or misusing another person's identifying information, or deceiving users online for financial gain (including phishing).
  • Cyber Libel & Online Exploitation: Unlawful online defamation and digital abuse.
How It Protects You: It empowers Philippine law enforcement agencies (PNP-ACG and NBI-CCD) to investigate digital crimes, apply for cybercrime warrants, preserve digital evidence, and prosecute offenders.
RA 10173
Data Privacy

Data Privacy Act of 2012

RA 10173 safeguards the fundamental human right to privacy while ensuring the free flow of information. It regulates how schools, businesses, and government agencies collect, store, and share your personal data.

Your Rights as a Data Subject

  • Right to Be Informed: You must be told why your personal data is being collected and how it will be used.
  • Right to Access: You can request a copy of the personal information an organization holds about you.
  • Right to Object: You can refuse the processing of your data for direct marketing or unauthorized purposes.
  • Right to Erasure or Blocking: You can request the removal of your data when it is no longer necessary or was unlawfully obtained.
  • Right to Rectification: You can ask organizations to correct inaccurate or outdated records.
  • Right to Damages: You may claim compensation if you suffer harm due to inaccurate, unlawfully used, or breached personal data.
72 hours

Mandatory Breach Notification

Under NPC rules, organizations must notify both the National Privacy Commission and affected individuals within 72 hours upon discovering a serious personal data breach.

RA 11934
SIM Registration

Subscriber Identity Module (SIM) Registration Act

Enacted to curb text scams, smishing, and anonymous mobile fraud, RA 11934 requires all mobile subscribers to register their SIM cards with their respective telecommunications providers.

How It Protects You: While it does not eliminate all scams overnight, it removes complete anonymity from local mobile numbers, helping authorities trace SIM cards linked to fraudulent activities and block reported scam numbers faster.
